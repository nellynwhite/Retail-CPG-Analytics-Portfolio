Every project is framed around the decision a category or vendor team has to make, and what the numbers say they should do about it.

Headline read
Growth is healthy and volume driven, but two problems are leaving money on the table: an in stock gap worth $4.1M in lost sales, and an OTIF compliance gap that is almost entirely an in full (shortage) problem rather than a delivery-timing one. Both are addressable with replenishment and order-accuracy fixes, not new demand.

Data model

Fact_WeeklySales

Week × Store × Item · 26,000 rows · TY/LY sales, units, margin, promo flag

Fact_Inventory

Week × Store × Item · 26,000 rows · on-hand, lost sales, weeks of supply, fill rate

Fact_PromoCalendar

Week × Item · 520 rows · lift, incremental units, trade spend, ROI

Fact_SupplyChain

Week × Item · 520 PO lines · OTIF, on-time, in full, shortage, fine exposure

Dim_Store

600 stores across 5 regions, formats and volume tiers

Dim_Item

10 SKUs across 5 categories, with base price and velocity

Dim_Supplier

5 suppliers, each with a contractual OTIF target

Dim_Date

NRF 52-week fiscal calendar, TY and LY weeks pre aligned

Competency areas

Analytics & BI

Power BI star schema (Import mode); 77 DAX measures across 6 display folders; NRF 52-week fiscal calendar patterns; price vs. volume YoY decomposition.

Data engineering

MySQL views, CTEs and window functions; star schema of 4 fact and 7 dimension tables; CSV to SQL to Power BI ETL with referential integrity enforced.

CPG domain

Walmart Scintilla vendor logic; OTIF measured to the Walmart standard (on time and in full); trade promo mechanics; in stock, fill rate and lost sales quantification.

Decision framing

Every dashboard ends in a recommendation. Findings are tied to a dollar impact and a named owner action, in the format a vendor review uses.
