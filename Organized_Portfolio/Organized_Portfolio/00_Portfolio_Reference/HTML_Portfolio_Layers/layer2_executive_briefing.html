This briefing translates a full year of Walmart Scintilla-style analytics into four actionable business areas: sales performance, inventory risk, promotional effectiveness, and supply chain reliability. Each section follows the standard CPG vendor review format — observation, implication, recommended action.

Headline findings
Sales Performance +6.8% YoY
What happened
Total sales reached $32.2M TY vs. $30.2M LY. Beverages led growth at +8.0% YoY on strong velocity in the Energy Drink and Sparkling Water SKUs. Pet grew slowest at +4.0%.
What it means
Growth is primarily volume-driven. Average selling prices held flat across the portfolio, meaning unit velocity improvements are the core driver — a healthy signal for shelf productivity.
Recommended action
Prioritize ranging expansion in the Beverages category. Investigate whether Pet underperformance is a shelf placement issue or a velocity ceiling vs. category benchmarks.
Inventory & In-Stock 88.9% In-Stock
What happened
The portfolio averaged 88.9% in-stock rate against a 95% target. An estimated $4.1M in sales was lost to stockouts across 419K units, with 1.4 average weeks of supply — below the 2.0 WOS threshold.
What it means
A 6-point gap vs. target is meaningful: every percentage point of in-stock improvement translates directly to recovered lost sales. At the current run rate, the portfolio is leaving ~12.8% of achievable revenue on the shelf.
Recommended action
Focus replenishment review on the 10% of store/item pairs generating the highest lost sales concentration. Raise safety stock to 2.0 WOS minimum during promo weeks.
Promotional Effectiveness 19.5% avg lift
What happened
128 promo events ran across the portfolio TY with $496K in estimated trade spend. Average promo lift was 19.5% above baseline with 149K incremental units generated.
What it means
Promotions are driving incremental volume, but the 26.8% promo mix signals the portfolio may be dependent on promotional demand. Over-reliance on TPR can erode base velocity and train shoppers to wait for deals.
Recommended action
Evaluate which SKUs show baseline velocity erosion in weeks following promo events. Shift trade investment toward Feature + Display combos where the additive lift justifies the spend.
Supply Chain / OTIF 91.3% vs 93.4% goal
What happened
OTIF averaged 91.3% against the 93.4% Walmart target. Peak Snacks (88.4%) and PetJoy (89.9%) are the most exposed, with the highest shortage unit totals at 68K and 26K respectively.
What it means
Falling below Walmart's OTIF threshold triggers financial penalties and can jeopardize vendor shelf positioning. A 2.1 percentage point gap vs. goal represents meaningful fine exposure for affected suppliers.
Recommended action
Escalate Peak Snacks and PetJoy to a quarterly OTIF improvement review. Validate whether the ML forecast improvement (2.0% MAPE vs. 2.6% naive) reduces shortage exposure if adopted upstream.

Top 4 recommendations for next quarter
  • 01
    Fix in-stock before adding new promo events
    Running promotions on items that are already stocking out amplifies the problem — demand spikes with no inventory to fulfill. Conduct a reorder point reset across the 50 highest-risk store/item pairs before Q1 promo planning begins.
    Estimated opportunity: partial recovery of $4.1M lost sales
  • 02
    Shift trade spend from TPR to Feature + Display combos
    TPR is the most-used mechanic but has the lowest ROI per dollar of trade spend. Feature + Display combinations deliver an additive lift effect that outperforms either mechanic alone. Reallocating even 15% of trade spend to this combination improves incremental ROI without increasing total spend.
    Trade spend efficiency improvement
  • 03
    Escalate Peak Snacks and PetJoy OTIF to JBP agenda
    Both suppliers are below their contractual OTIF targets. At Walmart, OTIF misses generate financial penalties and erode vendor standing in annual shelf space negotiations. Placing OTIF recovery as a formal agenda item in the next Joint Business Planning cycle creates accountability and a documented improvement path.
    Fine avoidance; shelf position protection
  • 04
    Investigate Pet category shelf productivity vs. benchmarks
    Pet growing at +4.0% against a portfolio average of +6.8% suggests either a category-level headwind or a shelf placement issue at the store level. Request category-level market share data through the Scintilla portal to separate the two scenarios before making a ranging decision.
    Category growth opportunity identification
